# Further Graph Transformations

## Course and source priority

Year 12 Mathematics Advanced. The topic is planned as three lessons: (1) sketching trigonometric graphs, (2) periodic applications, (3) logarithmic scales. Only Lesson 1 is implemented.

Primary source: the supplied `12MA Further Graph Transformations.pdf`, 51 PDF pages including preliminaries. Page references below are printed booklet page numbers. The cover says Year 12 Mathematics Extension and the homework uses Cambridge Extension 1 references. Retain that supplied reference system while teaching the Advanced course. No Advanced textbook question mapping has been inferred.

The previous `Trig Graphs.pptx` supplies the quarter-period construction approach. Its statement that a is the amplitude is refined to |a|; signed coefficients can reflect a curve. The other two supplied decks inform the wider topic plan but are not needed for this lesson's questions. The supplied sequence image identifies trigonometric graphs as the first of three lessons.

## Approved Lesson 1 approach

Assume students know y=sin x, y=cos x and y=tan x, radians and general transformations. Amplitude and period are new. Use an early transformation hinge, short definitions, then spend most of the time sketching. Handle a horizontal translation separately: factor the argument, sketch the unshifted modified graph, then translate the underlying curve and keep the specified final domain.

The shared cover is followed by the objective and success criterion, then a short prerequisite recap. Fifteen slides in total, including cover and homework. Approximate timing: opening/recap/hinge 6 minutes; amplitude, period and construction method 8 minutes; Examples 1.8 and 1.9 about 8 minutes each; tangent explanation and Example 1.15 about 10 minutes; phase-shift explanation and Example 1.19 about 10 minutes; consolidation/homework 3 minutes. Remaining time is flexible.

Use y=a sin nx+k, y=a cos nx+k and y=a tan nx+k throughout. Colour code a orange (#b55224), n teal (#007f78) and k blue (#3b66a8); repeat those colours in the amplitude, midline and period explanations. The amplitude distance arrow is orange, the midline is blue and the period distance arrows are teal. The supplied booklet example prompts retain their original expressions and values.

Objective: We are learning to sketch modified trigonometric graphs.

Success criteria: I can sketch a modified trigonometric graph with its key features correctly labelled.

The exact prompts, numbering, numerical values, notation and domains are preserved in the slides. Additional definitions, illustrations and the hinge are not presented as booklet questions.

Example 1.8: the one-cycle points are (0,1),(π/4,3/2),(π/2,1),(3π/4,1/2),(π,1), repeated to 2π.

Example 1.9: successive quarter-period values from −π to π are −1,2,5,2,−1,2,5,2,−1. There are four x-intercepts: ±α and ±(π−α), where α=(1/2)arccos(2/3)≈0.4205. Exact intercept calculations are not demanded by the sketch prompt; place the crossings accurately without inventing simple π-fractions.

Example 1.15: although the source uses closed interval signs, the function is undefined at all four listed asymptotes, including the endpoints. Its domain is (−3π,−π) union (−π,π) union (π,3π). Each centre has guide points π/2 to the left and right at heights −2 and 2. Branches are drawn as separate paths, with no line across an asymptote.

Example 1.19: factor 2(x−π/3). Zeros: π/3,5π/6,4π/3,11π/6. Maxima: (7π/12,3),(19π/12,3). Minima: (π/12,−3),(13π/12,−3). Shift the underlying periodic curve and then restrict to [0,2π]; do not shift the required domain. The two arrows on the graph show a movement π/3 to the right with each point staying at the same height. Explain the translation in words, without coordinate-mapping notation. Final endpoint values are included because shifted quarter-period points do not generally land at the requested endpoints.

If time permits, Example 1.10 can be a verbal follow-up: y=4sin(2πx)−1 has amplitude 4, period 1, midline −1 and quarter-period 1/4. It is not a required fifth worked example.

## Hinge and response routine

Question: The graph of y=sin 2x is obtained from y=sin x by which transformation?

1. Horizontal dilation, factor 2: direct rather than reciprocal factor.
2. Vertical dilation, factor 2: input/output confusion.
3. Horizontal dilation, factor 1/2: correct.
4. Vertical dilation, factor 1/2: reciprocal applied to the wrong coordinate.

Every original x-coordinate halves and the y-coordinate stays fixed: (π/2,1) maps to (π/4,1).

Use the adjustable 60-second timer. Students start with a fist on their chest, think quietly and show 1–4 fingers simultaneously at chest height on the teacher's verbal cue. Reveal the answer deliberately after collecting responses. Timer expiry does not reveal the answer. Reset clears answer visibility, selection and timer. Leaving the slide or opening the slide map pauses its timer.

Ready: continue to amplitude and period, connecting the compressed graph to its shorter period. Divided: pairs explain the mapped point, then re-poll. Further explanation needed: map (0,0),(π/2,1),(π,0), then check the corresponding horizontal factor 1/3 for y=cos 3x.
